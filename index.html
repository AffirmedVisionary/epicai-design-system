Colour

Two layers. The base palette belongs to everyone — all five colours show up across all four branches. Then each branch gets one signature colour that is entirely their own. That's what makes them feel like a family without looking the same.

★
How to use this

Typography

Three fonts, three jobs. Cormorant Garamond is the editorial one — it matches the logo wordmark and carries the weight. Montserrat keeps things crisp for UI and labels. Inter handles body copy. They each have a lane. Don't mix them randomly.

The logo

Two forms — full lockup (pinwheel + wordmark) and wordmark only. Up to 32 versions across four branch planet colours and two colourways. Use SVG for everything digital. PNG only when a platform forces it.

Two-colourway rule

  • Light surface (Parchment, Navajo White, white) — Prussian Blue wordmark. This is the default.
  • Dark surface (Prussian Blue, deep colour, photography) — Parchment wordmark.
  • Those PNG logos that looked black? The PNG had no transparency, so it defaulted to black in preview. The logo itself is unchanged — the background is whatever you place it on.
  • Prussian Blue on a true black surface is a contrast failure. Switch to Parchment.

Why SVG over PNG
SVG stays sharp whether it's a favicon or a billboard. Change its colour with one CSS variable. PNG bakes in the background — separate file for every size, blurry when scaled up. Use SVG for everything digital. PNG only when a platform won't let you do otherwise (some email clients, some social uploads).

Pattern: epic-ai-[asset]-[branch]-[background].ext

★
Parent logo vs. branch logo
Parent logo is for the agency as a whole — homepage, email header, about page, press. Once you're inside a branch (an Indigo tutorial, a Pixi prompt guide, an Eve newsletter post), switch to that branch's planet. It helps people know where they are inside the ecosystem.

Who we're building for — Maya

Maya is not a persona someone invented in a workshop. She is drawn from real clients, early community members, and the version of Charlene that existed three or four years ago. Every colour, font choice, and word exists with her in mind.

"You were not built wrong. You were using a system built for someone else."
The sentence Maya has been waiting her whole life to hear — EPIC AI Agency
Name
Maya — The Determined Builder
Age
28–45 · most commonly early to mid-30s
Situation
Solo founder or side-hustler. Building alone. Often a parent.
Experience
Likely ADHD, autism, dyslexia, or a combination. May have a chronic health condition.
Location
UK, US, Canada, or Australia. Works from home.
Digital
Comfortable with Canva and social. Has heard of AI. Not using it strategically — yet.
Pain point
Every system she tries was built for a neurotypical brain with unlimited energy. She blames herself. The problem is the fit.
Desire
A system that works with her brain. Practical AI capability. Permission to lead with her work.
!
Always write frontstage
Write for Maya specifically. Use real words — capacity, variable energy, brain differences, the restart cycle. The second you generalise to reach everyone, you reach no one. The wider audience does not feel excluded; they self-select in. Writing for her is an invitation, not a gate.
